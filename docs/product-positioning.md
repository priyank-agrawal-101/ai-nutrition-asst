## 3. Name, tagline, and brand meaning

### Gusto NutriBite

**Gusto NutriBite** represents the idea that understanding nutrition can be enjoyable, practical, and relevant to everyday life. The name brings together the enjoyment of food and the importance of understanding what we eat.

Nutrition should not feel like a restrictive diet, a complicated science, or a daily chore. The brand makes room for curiosity, familiar foods, culture, convenience, and personal choice. Its role is to help people make informed choices without losing the pleasure of eating.

For the product defined in the problem statement, these ideas come to life through approachable food, nutrition, and food-safety conversations. The brand meaning guides how the assistant explains information and relates to the user.

### Gusto — the joy of eating

**Gusto** is an Italian-derived word associated with taste, enjoyment, enthusiasm, and appreciation. It represents the emotional and experiential side of food.

Eating involves more than calories, nutrients, or numbers. It also includes preferences, culture, convenience, and personal choice. Gusto recognises that enjoyment is an important part of the everyday food experience.

For the brand, the guiding idea is that **healthy eating should complement the joy of eating, not take it away**. The assistant expresses this through useful, non-judgemental explanations that respect familiar foods and individual choices.

### NutriBite — understanding nutrition, one bite at a time

**NutriBite** combines two ideas:

- **Nutri** refers to nutrition: understanding foods, nutrients, and their nutritional value.
- **Bite** refers to a small, familiar part of eating and to information presented in manageable pieces.

Together, NutriBite connects everyday meals, snacks, and ingredients with the opportunity to understand what we consume. It represents breaking complex nutritional information into small, practical, easy-to-understand insights.

In the actual product, one question about a food, nutrient, cooking method, or storage practice can lead to a useful explanation. "One bite at a time" is the learning approach; it does not imply food logging or intake tracking.

### Meaning of the full name

Together, **Gusto NutriBite** brings enjoyment and nutritional awareness into one concept: **enjoy your food while understanding it better**.

Gusto supplies the emotional connection to food. NutriBite supplies the practical promise of understandable nutrition information. The full name expresses the relationship between those two ideas, rather than describing a tracker, diet planner, or clinical service.

### Meaning of the tagline

**Your personal AI nutrition companion** describes how the product makes that connection useful in a conversation.

| Part | Meaning for Gusto NutriBite |
| --- | --- |
| Your personal | Relevant to your question and the context of the active conversation, with explanations that meet you at your level of understanding. |

| Companion | A friendly, approachable way to ask questions and explore follow-ups without judgement. |

Here, personal assistance does not mean a nutrition profile, an individual medical assessment, calorie or weight targets, or a personalised diet prescription. The assistant's role remains the educational conversation in the brief, with appropriate referral outside its limits.

## 4. Premise, purpose, mission, and vision

### Premise from the problem statement

An AI model can give a confident food or nutrition answer without evidence. It may invent an attribution or give a different number when asked again. A polished answer can look credible even when its claims are unsupported.

Gusto NutriBite addresses this problem through a focused question-and-answer experience, structured claims, explicit safety boundaries, and recorded failures. The first milestone makes the absence of retrieved evidence visible. The second introduces retrieval and compares results using the same evaluation questions. Neither structured output nor a citation alone proves correctness.

The audience hypothesis is that everyday adults will value clear explanations that fit familiar food contexts and make uncertainty understandable. This is a hypothesis to learn from, not an invented research result.

**Purpose:** Help people enjoy food while understanding it better.

**Mission:** Make food, nutrition, and food-safety questions easier to understand through clear conversational explanations, transparent source states, and responsible boundaries.

**Long-term vision:** Become a trusted everyday companion that helps people understand food and make informed nutritional decisions throughout their lives. This vision guides the quality of the existing product; it does not add a new feature roadmap.

### Brand philosophy

| Principle | Meaning for this product |
| --- | --- |
| Enjoyment over restriction | Respect the pleasure, culture, and practicality of eating. Avoid food guilt and unnecessary rigid rules. |
| Understanding over confusion | Explain food concepts in clear, manageable language rather than presenting confident but opaque conclusions. |
| Context over one-size-fits-all explanations | Respond to the actual question, recipe, preparation, and conversational context without inventing personal prescriptions. |
| Consistency over perfection | Review changing facts and numbers across responses, preserve failures, and improve the product without promising perfect answers. |
| Guidance over judgement | Help people understand information without shaming their food choices or bodies. |

### Brand personality

| Quality | How it should feel |
| --- | --- |
| Friendly | A helpful companion that welcomes ordinary questions. |
| Intelligent | Useful explanations and relevant follow-ups, with no need for impressive-sounding jargon. |
| Approachable | Understandable to beginners and people already familiar with nutrition terms. |
| Positive | Supportive of curiosity and informed choice, without promising body transformation or health outcomes. |
| Trustworthy in conduct | Honest about estimates, uncertainty, sources, errors, and the distinction between general education and medical advice. |

Avoid a clinical, judgemental, restrictive, or calorie-and-weight-obsessed identity. Trust is earned through behaviour; it is not a certification implied by the name.

## 5. Positioning and unique value proposition

### Positioning statement

For everyday adults in India who have questions about food, nutrition, cooking, and food safety, **Gusto NutriBite** is an AI nutrition companion that offers understandable, contextual explanations while making evidence limitations and safety boundaries clear. Its experience combines the joy and familiarity of everyday food with a disciplined approach to structured answers, uncertainty, and learning from failures.

The category is an AI-powered nutrition assistant. The relationship is companion-like; the function is educational question answering. This positioning follows the problem statement rather than the broader feature list in the brand reference.

### Unique value proposition

**Make sense of everyday food questions through friendly, contextual explanations that are clear about their sources and limits.**

| Pillar | Customer value | Product expression | Evidence to learn from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Understand food, one question at a time | Make unfamiliar concepts more approachable. | Clear answers, useful definitions, and relevant follow-ups. | Comprehension observations and answer-quality reviews. |
| Keep the joy and familiarity of eating | Feel respected rather than judged. | Familiar Indian food examples and neutral language about bodies, cuisines, and preferences. | Feedback on relevance, tone, and unhelpful assumptions. |
| See the limits of the answer | Distinguish explanation from evidence and know when the request needs qualified advice. | Empty source states in Milestone 1, citations from retrieval in Milestone 2, explicit uncertainty, and appropriate refusals. | Unsupported claims, invented attributions, harmful answers, and source-display findings. |

These pillars describe the intended distinction, not a claim that no competitor offers them. The problem statement's structured claims, repeated-question comparisons, and failure logging provide a concrete improvement method. They do not make the first version more accurate by themselves.

The commercial learning question is whether this focused experience is more useful for the intended jobs than existing search, educational sites, or general AI tools. Findings can guide ongoing improvements.

## 6. Audience and India-first principles

The confirmed market direction is broad. The recommended first segment is narrower: **English-comfortable adults in India who regularly make household food choices and want explanations rather than prescribed targets.** This provides a coherent starting point for discovery and pilot recruitment.

Secondary segments include people learning to read packaged-food labels and adults beginning to shop or cook independently. Evaluators and maintainers are internal roles, not customer personas.

India-first should change the experience in concrete ways:

- Use familiar food terms such as dal, curd/dahi, poha, and idli when relevant, while recognising regional and recipe differences.
- Include vegetarian, non-vegetarian, and other dietary contexts without treating one as the default for everyone.
- Avoid equating Indian users with a single language, religion, cuisine, income, or city type. Test with varied participants who fit the initial language scope.
- Use metric units where appropriate. Do not treat “one bowl” or “one serving” as a reliable standard when recipes and sizes differ.
- Design for mobile web, readable text, ordinary connections, and interruptions. Describe performance using actual measurements rather than assumptions.
- Use Indian English initially. Hindi, Hinglish, and regional-language support are not current product claims.
- Avoid assuming access to specialist ingredients, premium brands, fitness devices, or private nutrition consultations. Referrals should remain respectful about access constraints.

Clinical nutrition needs, body-change targets, children as a primary customer segment, and professional decision support are outside the launch proposition. General permitted education remains available under the safety policy; this audience definition is not an age-verification system.

Global expansion requires more than replacing Indian food examples. Reassess language comprehension, source coverage, food terminology, units, privacy obligations, support, and safety referrals for each market. Do not advertise worldwide suitability from an English interface alone.

## 8. Jobs, use cases, and boundaries

| Priority | Job | Example starting question | Desired outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core | Understand a food or nutrient concept | “What does dietary fibre mean?” | The user can explain the concept in their own words. |
| Core | Understand preparation and ingredients | “Why does the recipe affect the nutrition of a dish?” | The user understands which information varies and why a single answer may be misleading. |
| Core | Interpret label terminology | “How is a serving different from 100 g?” | The user can distinguish the measurement bases without receiving a personal target. |
| Core, with added safety review | Learn general food handling and storage principles | “What information matters when deciding how to store leftovers?” | Useful education without unsupported assurance about a specific food's safety. |
| Secondary | Understand uncertainty in a general food claim | “What would count as good evidence for a nutrition claim?” | The user recognises the limits of an explanation and of the available sources. |
| Out of scope | Obtain a calorie or weight target, treatment, or condition-specific diet | “How many calories should I eat?” | A clear boundary and appropriate professional referral. |

A successful experience may be a short answer, a useful clarification, or a well-understood referral. It should not require a long conversation. Safe refusal is necessary, but frequent refusals among the intended segment may indicate poor positioning or a product that does not meet that segment's needs.

The supplied brand reference does not add meal planning, nutrition tracking, food-image recognition, profiles, or personalised diet guidance. The existing product scope and safety limits remain the source of truth.

## 10. Experience promise and customer journey

The initial experience should help someone move from a question to understanding. It should not frame food as a score to maximise or force account creation before the first useful interaction under the current proposed scope.

| Moment | User needs | Experience and message principle | What to learn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | Understand what the app is for. | Explain everyday education, show a relevant example, and state key limits. | Can people correctly describe what it can and cannot do? |
| Arrive | Know whether their question fits and how data is handled. | Visible scope/source notice and accessible privacy information before submission. | Are users surprised by exclusions or storage? |
| Ask | Express a question without completing a health profile. | A plain input, useful starter questions, and clear validation. | Which questions are abandoned or rejected, and why? |
| Wait | Know whether work is still in progress. | Honest pending state; recovery follows the existing contract. | Actual latency, failures, and comprehension of recovery actions. |
| Read | Understand the explanation and its limits. | Readable answer, visible source state, and no unsupported verification signals. | Can the user explain the answer and recognise uncertainty? |
| Follow up | Clarify the same topic. | Preserve the active conversation within its supported limits. | Do follow-ups improve understanding? |
| Encounter a boundary | Understand why the request is outside scope. | Brief, respectful refusal and relevant referral or separate permitted question. | Does the user understand the boundary without feeling blamed? |
| Leave or return | Control when to use the product again. | Support episodic use; accurately explain session/history limits. | Is it useful when another food question arises? |

The [UX flows](ux-flow.md) own exact interaction behaviour. Do not add streaks, calorie rings, weight charts, alarming health scores, or engagement prompts just to make the app feel more substantial.

## 13. Content and copy style guide

### Voice

Use plain, calm, practical language. Write as a knowledgeable explainer who respects the reader's judgement. Be warm through clarity and helpfulness, without excessive enthusiasm, flattery, or pretending to be a person with lived experience.

For permitted answers, start with the direct explanation. Add the context needed to avoid misunderstanding, acknowledge uncertainty where it matters, and offer a relevant follow-up only when useful. Exact assistant templates and answer-length rules belong in `prompt-spec.md`.

Do not start every answer with a disclaimer. Keep the persistent product notice findable and place specific boundaries or uncertainty at the point where they matter. A refusal should state the limit early; a safety-sensitive answer must not hide qualifications at the end.

### Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone and approach | Example to prefer | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First visit | Welcoming and specific. | “Ask a question about food, nutrition, or food safety.” | “Transform your health with the ultimate AI nutritionist.” |
| Ordinary explanation | Direct and understandable. | “The meaning depends on the serving size shown on the label.” | “Unlock optimal dietary intelligence.” |
| Missing context | Ask for relevant, non-sensitive context. | “Which term on the label would you like explained?” | Asking for weight or medical history to answer a general label question. |
| Uncertainty | Explain the reason and consequence. | “Recipes differ, so one number may not describe the dish you mean.” | “It depends” without explanation, or an invented precise value. |
| Prohibited request | Respectful boundary and referral. | “I can't recommend a calorie target. A qualified dietitian can help with individual needs.” | Giving the target after “This is not medical advice.” |
| Service failure | Calm, concrete recovery. | “Couldn't complete the answer. Try again.” | “Oops! Our nutrition brain got confused.” |
| Return visit | Useful without pressure. | “Ask a new food question.” | “You missed your streak” or guilt about absence. |

Urgent-risk wording must follow the safety policy and approved templates. Do not invent emergency numbers, diagnose an emergency, or encourage someone to wait for the app.

### Editorial conventions

- Use Indian English with British spelling for product-authored copy: “fibre,” “personalised,” and “colour.” Preserve source titles, product labels, quotations, and code identifiers as written.
- Use sentence case for headings, buttons, and labels. Brand spelling is Gusto NutriBite; the tagline is “Your personal AI nutrition companion.”
- Prefer common words. Define necessary terms at first use; do not assume nutrition literacy.
- Prefer short paragraphs and descriptive headings. Use lists for steps and tables only when a comparison is easier to understand that way.
- Address the reader as “you.” Use “we” only for real operator responsibilities. Never suggest a human professional has reviewed the current answer unless that is true.
- Use metric units by default when a quantity is appropriate. Explain unfamiliar units. Do not convert vague household portions into exact quantities without adequate information.
- Distinguish an estimate, range, example, and established measurement. Numerical precision must reflect the evidence and recipe context.
- Explain regional synonyms where helpful, such as “curd (dahi).” Do not repeatedly define familiar terms or treat one regional name as universal.
- Preserve dignity across food preferences, religion, affordability, disability, literacy, and body size. Do not infer these traits from a question.
- Use descriptive link text and accessible labels. Never make colour, an emoji, or an icon the only carrier of meaning.
- Avoid exclamation-heavy copy, unnecessary emoji, fear-based urgency, and emotional language that makes a user dependent on the app.

### Vocabulary and framing

| Prefer | Avoid or restrict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI nutrition companion, explanation, general education | AI dietitian, doctor, clinician, personal nutritionist | The app does not provide professional assessment or care. |
| Food choices, preparation, context | Clean/dirty food, cheat meal, guilt-free, good/bad person | Food language should not moralise behaviour. |
| Sources, supporting material, uncertainty | Verified, proven, guaranteed, expert-approved | Use only when the specific claim is supportable. |
| General information | Your ideal diet, perfect macros, optimal weight | These suggest excluded or unsupported personal prescriptions. |
| Clear next action | Optimise, revolutionise, supercharge, unlock | Familiar verbs make the experience easier to understand. |
| A factual description of the recipe or label | Detox, miracle food, fat-burning, disease reversal | Avoid unsupported health and body-change claims. |

Do not call a source “research” merely because a model names an institution. In Milestone 1, no retrieved citations are available. In later versions, source quality and relevance still need review.

| Confirmed failed attempt | “Couldn't complete the answer. Try again.” / **Retry** | Retry follows a confirmed failure. |
| Unknown outcome | “We couldn't confirm whether your answer completed.” / **Check again** | Check status rather than silently generating another answer. |
| New conversation | **New chat** | Starts a conversation; does not delete stored data. |
| Excluded request | “I can't recommend a calorie target. A qualified dietitian can help with individual needs.” | State the boundary and an appropriate referral without supplying the prohibited advice. |
| Mixed request | State the boundary and referral, then offer a separate general question. | Follow the policy's response rule. |

Data-control copy should describe the actual control and its scope. The proposed session-wide deletion design remains documented in [safety and security](safety-and-security.md).

### FAQ copy

**What can I ask?** General questions about food, nutrition, cooking, storage, and food safety within the published limits.

**What does “personal” mean?** The assistant responds to your question and can use the active conversation for follow-ups. It does not assess your medical needs, prescribe a personal diet, or recommend calorie or weight targets.

**Can it plan meals or track nutrition?** Those features are not part of the current product scope. Gusto NutriBite is a food-and-nutrition question-answering assistant.

**Can it tell me what to eat for a condition?** No. It does not provide medical advice or condition-specific diets. A qualified healthcare professional or dietitian can help with individual advice.

**Where do answers come from?** The first planned version uses the model's knowledge without retrieved sources. Answers may be wrong. Milestone 2 adds retrieval, with citations and remaining evidence gaps shown accurately.

**What happens to my questions?** The planned service stores conversation data and sends the context needed for a response to the selected model provider. The product's privacy notice describes the actual provider, retention, deletion, and support details.

**Is it free?** Pricing is undecided. A limited free pilot is a commercial recommendation, not a commitment to unlimited or permanently free access.

## 15. Content strategy and editorial practice

Content should help someone understand a real food question. Focus on the jobs in the brief and the audience's everyday context, rather than traffic or engagement alone.

| Pillar | Example theme | Editorial approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nutrition in understandable pieces | Nutrient roles, common terms, and general educational questions. | Explain concepts without converting them into personal targets. |
| Familiar food and cooking | Why ingredients, recipes, and preparation affect an explanation. | Recognise variation across dishes and regions. |
| Food safety and storage | General principles and the context needed to understand them. | Avoid unsupported assurance that a particular food is safe. |
| Claims and uncertainty | What a nutrition claim says, what evidence would help, and what remains unclear. | Explain uncertainty without invented attributions or empty hedging. |
| How the assistant works | Sources, conversation context, safety limits, and data controls. | Describe the actual product version. |

Editorial work can combine topic selection, source checks, drafting, human review, and corrections. For high-consequence food-safety or nutrition statements, suitable subject-matter review helps identify errors. Do not imply that a review or endorsement occurred when it did not.

Assess source relevance and quality, preserve important qualifications, and record dates where they help readers understand recency. Marketing articles need sound factual support even though Milestone 1's generated answers have no retrieval. Avoid unreviewed mass-produced claims, copied creator content, fabricated credentials, and promotional recommendations disguised as education.

Keep an owner and review date for maintained content. Factual corrections, source changes, and changes to product behaviour are useful prompts for revision. This is an ongoing editorial practice, not a product launch checklist.

## 16. Implications for the Design Guide

Design can proceed from the selected Gusto NutriBite identity. The brand should feel friendly, intelligent, approachable, and positive, while making evidence and limitations easy to understand.

| Brand principle | Design implication | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joy of eating | Warm, food-aware imagery and a welcoming tone. | Does the experience respect ordinary food choices without guilt or body-transformation cues? |

| Personal companionship | Conversational context, approachable copy, and useful follow-ups. | Does the interface feel helpful without suggesting clinical care or a nutrition profile? |
| Transparent limits | Findable source states, clear refusals, and distinct service errors. | Can users distinguish missing evidence, a safety boundary, and a failed request? |
| India-first relevance | Varied familiar foods and realistic cooking/shopping contexts. | Do examples avoid a single regional, premium, or stereotyped diet? |
| Accessibility | Keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, responsive text, and understandable status updates. | Can users read and operate the experience across mobile and desktop? |

The Design Guide will define typography, colour, spacing, components, icons, imagery, responsive layouts, focus, motion, and the existing conversation/source/error states. WCAG 2.2 AA remains the accessibility target, not an untested compliance claim.

## 18. Business model and sustainability

A **limited free pilot** is a reasonable starting option. Pricing, usage allowances, and operating budget remain open commercial choices, not dependencies introduced by this positioning document.

| Option | Opportunity to explore | Consideration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer payment for continued use | Could support a useful recurring educational service. | Willingness to pay is unknown; billing and accounts are outside the current implementation scope. |
| A non-clinical educational offering | Could serve a defined learning audience. | Different buyers and support needs may distract from the initial consumer product. |
| Advertising or affiliate revenue | Could reduce direct user fees. | Creates conflicts around food recommendations and sensitive context; not the recommended starting model. |

Commercial options do not add meal planning, tracking, personalised diets, or other features from the brand reference. Keep source honesty, safety, and privacy separate from paid benefits. Do not sell conversations or allow a sponsor to shape factual answers; disclose any real commercial relationship accurately.

Explore value and price sensitivity in INR for the India-first audience. No amount, package, traffic forecast, or margin is assumed. Research can inform pricing as the product develops without being a launch condition.

Service economics include all model calls, retrieval where present, infrastructure, monitoring, support, editorial review, and evaluation. An answer may involve multiple provider calls under the [architecture](architecture.md), so counting only visible answer generation understates cost. Use the [efficiency guidance](token-optimization-and-efficiency.md) to reduce waste without weakening quality or safety.

Track variable cost per attempted question and cost per reviewed useful outcome. If payments are later introduced, revenue and contribution after variable service costs can complement these measures.

## 19. Success measures and measurement boundaries

The proposed north-star measure is **the proportion of reviewed, eligible food-question tasks resolved usefully**. It reflects the educational job rather than conversation length or daily dependence.

For a defined review set, the denominator is every reviewed in-scope task, including failed or abandoned attempts. The numerator is tasks with a relevant, understandable explanation judged factually adequate for the job and without an unresolved harmful or misleading-evidence issue. The detailed rubric belongs in `evaluation.md`. Report sample size, selection method, and review coverage.

| Measure | Definition or use | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User-reported usefulness | Share of optional responses reporting that an explanation helped. | Report response rate; perceived usefulness does not prove factual accuracy. |
| Comprehension | Whether a participant can explain the answer and relevant limitation in their own words. | Use observed tasks rather than inferring understanding from clicks. |
| Eligible-task resolution | The numerator and denominator above. | Include failures and abandoned tasks rather than selecting only successful answers. |
| Unsupported claims and invented sources | Findings recorded during answer review. | Directly addresses the problem statement's central concern. |
| Consistency | Factual or numerical changes when the same question is repeated. | Compare substance, not just wording. |
| Safety findings and unnecessary refusals | Prohibited advice that is given, and permitted questions that are incorrectly refused. | Record both safety and usefulness failures; zero observed failures is not universal assurance. |
| Evidence honesty | Whether source display and attribution reflect actual support. | A citation is not itself proof of a claim. |
| Completion and latency | Completed attempts, failures, recoveries, and measured response times. | Distinguish answers, refusals, and service failures. |
| Cost and support effort | Cost per attempt/useful outcome and staff effort over a stated period. | Include review work and non-generation provider calls. |
| Return for another relevant need | Voluntary research feedback or appropriately scoped usage measures. | Episodic usefulness does not require daily use. |

The brief's ten fixed questions, three-response comparison, and preserved failure log remain the core evaluation work. Broader product measures complement them; they do not replace the required checks or create new launch gates.

The draft browser session lasts seven days and there are no accounts. That identity cannot establish thirty-day user retention, cross-device behaviour, or unique-person counts. Do not add fingerprinting to fill the gap. Separately consented research can help explore longer-term usefulness.

No analytics tool, feedback widget, or transcript export is added here. Prefer minimum necessary aggregate events and keep real visitor data separate from synthetic evaluation. Data handling follows the [safety and security document](safety-and-security.md). Numerical growth or conversion targets are not invented in this draft.

## 20. Research and learning opportunities

Research can run alongside design and development. It is not a prerequisite for using the selected name, completing another document, or launching the product.

An optional starting exercise is 8–12 qualitative interviews, mainly with the primary persona and some participants in adjacent situations. Seek varied food habits, locations, budget constraints, and technical confidence within the language scope. This would be a discovery sample, not a representative survey.

Ask about a recent real food question, the answer-seeking process, what remained confusing, and what the participant found useful. Avoid collecting medical histories or encouraging sensitive disclosures. Separate observed behaviour from reactions to a concept.

| ID | Learning question | Method | Potential improvement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HYP-01 | Are everyday food explanations a recurring useful job? | Discuss recent examples and existing alternatives. | Refine starter questions and audience emphasis. |
| HYP-02 | Do familiar Indian examples improve understanding? | Observe comprehension with comparable explanations. | Improve cultural relevance and reduce assumptions. |
| HYP-03 | Does a focused assistant add value beyond search and general AI? | Compare equivalent in-scope tasks and preserve unfavourable findings. | Improve explanations, follow-ups, or the source experience. |
| HYP-04 | Do people understand the educational boundary? | Ask what help they expect from the assistant and from a professional. | Clarify scope copy and acquisition messaging. |
| HYP-05 | Does “personal AI nutrition companion” communicate contextual assistance? | Observe reactions to the chosen identity in real interface copy. | Explain “personal” more clearly without reopening the selected name or tagline. |
| HYP-06 | Do people understand the absence of sources in Milestone 1? | Ask what supports an answer and what remains uncertain. | Improve the notice and source-state presentation. |
| HYP-07 | What value and economics could sustain the product? | Explore recurring needs, actual alternatives, and service cost. | Refine commercial options without changing safety boundaries. |

A research log can record the question, method, consent, observations, counterexamples, and resulting changes. Minimise personal data and distinguish actual findings from hypotheses. Do not repurpose private visitor conversations as public research examples.

Task-based feedback can include an ordinary question, missing sources, an excluded request, and a service failure. Use findings to improve the experience continuously.

## 21. Product risks and ownership

| Risk | Product implication | Ongoing response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Personal companion” is understood as clinical or prescriptive | People may expect prohibited advice. | Explain the educational role through adjacent copy and consistent answers. |
| The experience adds little beyond existing tools | Low repeat usefulness or willingness to pay. | Compare actual jobs and improve the focused experience. |
| Source-free answers are mistaken for verified facts | Users may overtrust an unsupported answer. | Keep source limitations visible and preserve factual failures in evaluation. |
| India-first becomes a narrow cultural stereotype | Irrelevant examples or exclusion. | Use varied food contexts and review assumptions. |
| Food enjoyment becomes a promise of health improvement | Misleading product expectations. | Keep the emotional benefit distinct from medical or outcome claims. |
| The brand reference is mistaken for the feature roadmap | Meal planning, tracking, or diet features enter scope unintentionally. | Refer feature decisions back to the problem statement and product specification. |
| Marketing outpaces implementation | Descriptions conflict with the actual product. | Update copy as capabilities and source support change. |
| Service costs exceed useful value | Unsustainable delivery. | Track full service costs and consider bounded usage and suitable pricing. |

Useful ownership areas are product/brand, engineering/operations, privacy/support, and editorial quality. One person can hold several roles.

The product owner maintains positioning. Engineering records implemented behaviour. Editorial review helps assess factual wording and uncertainty. The operator handles support and incident procedures through the appropriate technical documents. Do not imply an expert panel, professional review of every answer, or a guaranteed support window unless those arrangements actually exist.
